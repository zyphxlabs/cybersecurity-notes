## How Attacks Started. Early Malware

**Brain Virus. 1986**

- The Brain Virus was created by the Alvi brothers to track pirated software.

- It spread through infected disks and jumped to every new computer it came into contact with.

- The Brain Virus did not destroy any data. It slowed everything down globally within a few months.

- This was the first wake up call for organizations. They realized they needed a security plan to protect themselves from these kinds of attacks.

**Morris Worm. 1988**

- The Morris Worm was built by Robert Morris to measure the size of the internet.

- However a bug in the worm caused it to re-install itself on the machines over and over again until they crashed.

- The Morris Worm took down 6,000 computers. Which was 10% of the internet at the time.

- The attack cost millions of dollars in damages.

- As a result of this attack, Computer Emergency Response Teams or CERTs were created to respond to these kinds of threats.

## Internet Age Attacks

**LoveLetter. 2000**

- The LoveLetter virus spread via email with the subject "I Love You" and an attachment called "Love Letter For You".

- When people opened the attachment it scanned their contacts. Sent itself to everyone in their address book.

- The LoveLetter virus infected 45 million computers. Caused $10 billion in damages.

- This was the major example of social engineering at scale. It showed how attackers could use psychological tricks to get people to do what they wanted.

**Equifax Breach. 2017**

- Attackers hit the credit reporting agency Equifax.

- They stole 143 million customer records. Including social security numbers, birth dates, addresses and credit card numbers.

- The breach was not caused by one vulnerability.. By multiple known issues that were never patched.

- Equifax paid $575 million to settle with the US government.

- The breach put every company on notice about the real financial cost of ignoring vulnerabilities and not taking security seriously.

## Attack Types to Know

**Phishing. Tricking people through communications**

- There are many different types of phishing attacks. Including BEC or business email compromise, which impersonates a known source to get financial information.

- Spear phishing targets a specific person or group.

- Whaling is a type of spear phishing that is aimed at executives.

- Vishing uses voice calls to steal information.

- Smishing uses text messages to steal information.

**Malware. Software built to cause damage**

- A virus needs a user to open it. Then it hides in files and spreads.

- A worm spreads on its own without any user action.

- Ransomware encrypts your data and demands payment to unlock it.

- Spyware silently. Sells your data without your consent.

**Social Engineering. Exploiting behavior**

- Social media phishing researches the target on social media first.

- A watering hole attack targets a site that the target visits frequently.

- USB baiting leaves an USB drive for someone to find and plug in.

- Physical social engineering impersonates an employee or vendor to get physical access.

**Why Social Engineering Works**

- Attackers use different tactics to trick people. Including authority, intimidation, consensus, scarcity, familiarity and urgency.

- They try to make people feel like they have to act or that everyone else is already doing something.

- They build emotional connections with their targets.

- They make people feel like they are in charge. Or like they have to follow the rules.

## CISSP Eight Security Domains

**Domain 1. Security and Risk Management**

- This domain is about defining security goals, mitigating risk and making sure that organizations are compliant with regulations.

- It is also about business continuity. Making sure that organizations can keep running even if something bad happens.

- For example if a new regulation like HIPAA comes out organizations need to update their policies to make sure they are compliant.

**Domain 2. Asset Security**

- This domain is about securing and managing digital and physical assets.

- It is about making sure that sensitive information is protected. And that old hardware is properly destroyed.

- For example if an organization is getting rid of some computers they need to make sure that all of the sensitive data is wiped from the hard drives.

**Domain 3. Security Architecture and Engineering**

- This domain is about making sure that the right tools, systems and processes are in place to protect an organization.

- It is about configuring firewalls and other security systems to block traffic.

- For example an organization might need to configure a firewall to block employees from accessing websites.

**Domain 4. Communication and Network Security**

- This domain is about managing and securing physical networks and wireless communications.

- It is about blocking employees from connecting to public hotspots.

- For example an organization might need to set up a private network or VPN to protect employees who are working remotely.

**Domain 5. Identity and Access Management**

- This domain is about controlling who has access to what. Both physically and digitally.

- It is about setting up keycard access and managing user permissions.

- For example an organization might need to set up a system to track who is accessing buildings or rooms.

**Domain 6. Security Assessment and Testing**

- This domain is about running audits and testing controls to make sure that an organization is secure.

- It is about analyzing data to identify risks and vulnerabilities.

- For example an organization might need to audit who has access to payroll information.

**Domain 7. Security Operations**

- This domain is about investigating incidents and implementing preventative measures.

- It is about following a playbook to stop threats and respond to incidents.

- For example if an unknown device connects to an organizations network the security team needs to follow the playbook to stop the threat.

**Domain 8. Software Development Security**

- This domain is all about building coding practices into the development lifecycle.

- It is about advising development teams on password policies and other security measures.

- For example an organization might need to advise its development team on how to build a mobile app.

## Threat Actor Types

**APT. Advanced Persistent Threat**

- An APT is a skilled attacker who researches their targets in advance.

- They stay hidden inside networks for a time. Often months or even years.

- They target infrastructure, government agencies and intellectual property.

- APTs are very dangerous because they are so skilled and patient.

**Insider Threat**

- An insider threat is someone who has authorized access to an organizations systems and data.. Who abuses that access.

- They might be motivated by sabotage, espionage or financial gain.

- Insider threats can be very difficult to detect. Because they are already inside the organization.

**Hacktivist**

- A hacktivist is someone who's politically motivated. They use hacking to push an agenda or make a point.

- They might target government agencies, corporations or other organizations.

- Hacktivists often use social engineering tactics to get inside an organizations systems.

**Hacker Categories**

- There are different types of hackers. Including authorized, semi-authorized and unauthorized hackers.

- Authorized hackers are also known as hackers. They follow the law and find vulnerabilities in order to help organizations fix them.

- Semi-authorized hackers are researchers who find vulnerabilities but do not exploit them.

- Unauthorized hackers are malicious. They break the law. Sell stolen data for financial gain.

## Key Terms

- Social engineering is exploiting error to get access or information.

- Phishing is tricking people through communication.

- Malware is any software that is designed to harm devices or networks.

- A virus needs user action to spread. It hides in files. Spreads, to other computers.

- A worm spreads on its own without any user action.

- Ransomware encrypts data and demands payment to unlock it.

- Spyware silently collects and sells data without consent.

- BEC is business email compromise. It impersonates a known source to get information.

- A watering hole attack targets a site that the target visits frequently.

- USB baiting leaves an USB drive for someone to find and plug in.

- An APT is a persistent threat. A highly skilled and patient attacker.

- A CERT is a Computer Emergency Response Team. It was created after the Morris worm to respond to these kinds of threats.
