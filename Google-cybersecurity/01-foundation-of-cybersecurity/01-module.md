# Course 1 Module 1. What is Cybersecurity

## The Main Idea

Security is about making sure the right people can access the information and nobody else can.

The three main things security protects are called the CIA triad.

- Confidentiality means only authorized people can see the data.

- Integrity means the data stays accurate and unchanged.

- Availability means systems and data are accessible when needed.

## Two Types of Threats

- External threats come from someone outside the organization trying to get in.

- Internal threats come from current or former employees, vendors or trusted partners.

- Accidental threats happen when someone accidentally clicks a bad link or makes a mistake.

- Intentional threats happen when someone intentionally accesses systems without authorization or abuses systems on purpose.

## What Security Teams Do

Security teams do things.

- They respond to. Monitor networks when something looks off.

- They do pen testing, which's like ethically hacking their own organization to find weaknesses before threat actors do.

- They install prevention software with IT before threats happen.

- They do security audits to check records and verify who has access to what.

- They support dev teams so products ship without any security gaps.

- They ensure compliance by following regulations to avoid fines and audits.

- They maintain business continuity by keeping things running during a breach.

## Entry Level Analyst Day to Day

As an entry-level analyst your day-to-day tasks may include:

- Responding to alerts and doing investigations on the operations side.

- Building detections and improving existing ones on the project side.

- Writing playbooks, which are step-by-step guides on how to handle specific incidents.

## Transferable Skills

The skills you learn in security are transferable to other areas.

- Communication is key as you need to explain threats to people with no technical background.

- Collaboration is important as you work with engineers, forensic investigators and managers.

- Problem-solving skills are essential as you need to identify patterns and find the most efficient fix.

- Time management is critical as wrong priority order means more damage.

- Having a growth mindset is important as tactics change fast. You never stop learning in this field.

## Technical Skills to Build

To succeed in security you need to build some skills.

- Programming skills in languages like Python and SQL are helpful for automating repetitive tasks and searching for patterns.

- SIEM tools collect logs from everywhere and flag unusual activity like unknown logins.

- IDS monitors the network and alerts when something suspicious is detected.

- Incident response skills are critical as you need to follow the playbook investigate the alert and fix the root cause.

- Threat landscape knowledge is important as you need to stay current on what attackersre doing right now.

## Key Terms

Here are some terms to know:

- CIA Triad refers to Confidentiality, Integrity and Availability.

- A threat actor is any person or group that's a security risk.

- Security posture refers to how ready an organization is to defend and respond to threats.

- Compliance means following internal standards and external regulations.

- Security frameworks are guidelines for building risk mitigation plans.

- Security controls are actual safeguards that reduce specific risks.

- PII refers to personally identifiable information like name, email, phone and IP address.

- SPII refers to sensitive PII like SSN, medical records and biometrics which have stricter rules.

- Identity theft is stealing personal info to commit fraud and the main goal is money.

- Network security is keeping the organizations network infrastructure secure.

- Cloud security is making sure cloud assets are configured correctly and access is controlled.

## Why Security Actually Matters

A breach does not just hurt the company it hurts people.

- People lose money get their identity stolen and their reputation damaged.

- SPII leaks are worse, than PII leaks as they have bigger legal consequences and are harder to recover from.

- Security jobs are growing 30 percent by 2030 according to the US Bureau of Labor Statistics.
