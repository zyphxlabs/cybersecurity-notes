## Staying current in security

Security is a field that keeps changing all the time so its important to stay updated even after finishing this program because it shows hiring managers you are willing to keep learning on your own. OWASP top 10 is a good example of this since its a globally recognized document listing the top 10 most critical risks to web apps and it gets updated every three to four years which shows how the field keeps evolving

A few good resources to check periodically

- CSO Online for news analysis and research on security and risk management topics many CSOs use this for tips
- Krebs on Security an in depth blog by former Washington Post reporter Brian Krebs covering security news and investigations into cyber attacks
- Dark Reading covers topics like analytics application security mobile cloud security and IOT

## Engaging with security organizations and conferences

Before picking an organization or conference i should figure out what my actual interest is like do i want to react to incidents or prevent them am i into forensics or logging or do i want to be a CISO someday knowing this helps narrow down the search

Once i know my interest i can just search stuff like incident response cybersecurity conferences in my area or forensic security organizations in my area and get relevant results

LinkedIn is also useful for this searches like incident response cybersecurity groups or organizations for cybersecurity analysts can help find the right groups to join

Even here i need to stay cautious about social engineering since attackers use social media too to trick people so i should never click unexpected links or attachments from unfamiliar users

## Mailing lists

CISA offers two mailing lists worth signing up for

- one focused on threat info best practices and analysis from CISA domestic and international partners
- one that gives weekly summaries of new vulnerabilities that could risk an organizations network

## Building career connections on social media

Following CISOs on linkedin is a good move since they post interviews articles and share whats happening in the security space to find them i can just search their name if i know which company or org i want to work for then look them up on linkedin

Connecting with other analysts already working in the field is another good way to grow searching cybersecurity analysts on linkedin and filtering for people works well when sending a connection request a short note like asking about their experience as an analyst goes a long way

I can also filter linkedin for events and groups related to security topics im interested in

For people who arent big on social media joining a cybersecurity association is another route just searching cybersecurity industry associations online gives a bunch of options and i can pick ones that match my goals

## Sending good linkedin messages

When reaching out to someone new the message should sound conversational give a clear reason for connecting and be free of spelling or grammar mistakes this makes the person trust that im not a scammer trying to exploit them

Example format thats effective is something like introducing myself as someone who completed the program and wanting to connect with experienced professionals to learn from them keeping it short and genuine works best
