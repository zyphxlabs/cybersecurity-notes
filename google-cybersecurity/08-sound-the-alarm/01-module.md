## Security mindset

Security mindset is basically the ability to always keep evaluating risk and keep looking out for any breach that could happen or already happened in a system application or data. It is not just about knowing what to protect but also knowing who or what you are protecting it from. Every click has the potential to cause a breach so as an analyst you gotta think like that and prepare for worst case even if it never happens. Entry level analysts protect both low level assets like guest wifi and high level ones like PII trade secrets and financial info so basically the mindset covers everything not just the big stuff.

Staying updated with latest attacks and threats is part of building this mindset because new ideas for protecting data come from knowing what attackers are doing right now. Having this mindset can literally set you apart in interviews too.

## Data classification types

- Public data - already available to public and low risk but still needs basic protection like press releases job descriptions marketing stuff
- Private data - should be kept from public exposure like company emails employee ids research data if leaked can cause serious risk
- Sensitive data - includes PII SPII and PHI stuff like bank account numbers ssn passwords passport numbers medical info this one causes real financial and reputation damage if leaked
- Confidential data - stuff important for ongoing operations like trade secrets financial records government data often needs NDA to access

## Asset classification

Assets get labeled based on how sensitive or important they are to the company ranging from low to high level. Public data counts as low level asset since it wont hurt the org if its exposed. Sensitive and confidential data are high level assets because leaking them can cause loss of competitive edge reputation and trust. A company website is low level while an internal email talking about trade secrets is high level.

Every company has its own data classification policy and knowing that policy helps you understand what needs the most protection.

## Collaboration and reporting

No one person can handle security alone some people work on financial data some on passwords some on vendor security some on PII. All these people care about keeping the org and its customers safe. When a security event actually results in a breach its called a security incident but if it gets resolved without a breach its not an incident.

Even small stuff like an employee installing an app without permission should be escalated because that app might have vulnerabilities. Bigger stuff like malicious code found in a log can lead to downtime or big financial loss. Basically if unsure just report it to supervisor better safe than sorry.

## Business continuity and disaster recovery plans

These are the last two steps of a four part process the first two being identify assets and determine threats then implement detection tools and finally create these plans together.

Business continuity plan is a doc that explains how to keep business running during and after a disruption. Steps for it are

- Conduct business impact analysis to see what effects a disruption could have
- Identify document and implement recovery steps for critical functions
- Organize a business continuity team usually from cybersecurity IT HR communications and operations
- Conduct training for that team so they practice different risk scenarios

Disaster recovery plan is more about outlining steps to minimize damage from an actual incident like a ransomware attack and helps resolve the threat itself. Steps include

- Recovery strategies to restore software
- Recovery strategies to restore hardware
- Identifying what apps and data got impacted after the incident

Both plans are made together to minimize damage when something actually goes wrong.

## Juliana Soto case study

Juliana just got hired as cybersecurity analyst at a fictional payment company called Right On Time Payment Solutions. On day one she goes through company materials to understand what matters most to them. She realizes the company collects a lot of PII like usernames passwords full names EIN for business customers bank info and debit card numbers so she figures customer data is the most important asset here.

She then builds an information lifecycle strategy which has four steps

- Identify important assets like PII financial info ssn and EIN
- Assess current security measures and review company policies she cant do vuln scanning yet since shes new
- Protect the assets by shadowing a senior analyst since its day one
- Monitor the systems and report back to supervisor

Her supervisor was impressed and let her observe monitoring on her first day itself. This whole case basically shows how a new analyst should approach understanding a company and its assets from scratch.
