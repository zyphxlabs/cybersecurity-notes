## The Main Idea

Security is basically just making sure the right people can access information and nobody else can, and there are three things it protects called the CIA triad

- Confidentiality means only authorized people can see the data
- Integrity means the data stays accurate and nobody tampered with it
- Availability means the systems and data are actually accessible when you need them

## Two Types of Threats

Threats can come from outside or inside, external threats are someone outside the organization trying to get in, internal threats are current or former employees, vendors or trusted partners who already have access

Accidental threats happen when someone just makes a mistake like clicking a bad link, intentional threats are when someone on purpose accesses systems without authorization or abuses the access they already have

## What Security Teams Do

- Monitor networks and respond when something looks off
- Do pen testing which is basically ethically hacking their own organization to find weaknesses before the bad guys do
- Install prevention software with IT before threats even happen
- Run security audits to check records and verify who has access to what
- Support dev teams so products ship without security gaps
- Make sure the organization stays compliant with regulations to avoid fines
- Maintain business continuity so things keep running even during a breach

## Entry Level Analyst Day to Day

As an entry level analyst the work splits into two sides, on the operations side you respond to alerts and investigate them, on the project side you build detections, improve existing ones and write playbooks which are basically step by step guides on how to handle specific incidents

## Skills That Matter

Transferable skills matter a lot in this field, communication is huge because you have to explain threats to people who have no technical background at all, collaboration is important because you work with engineers, forensic investigators and managers, problem solving is essential because you need to find patterns and figure out the most efficient fix, time management is critical because wrong priority order means more damage, and having a growth mindset is just required because tactics change fast and you never stop learning

Technical skills you need to build:

- Programming in Python and SQL helps with automating repetitive tasks and searching for patterns
- SIEM tools collect logs from everywhere and flag unusual activity like unknown logins
- IDS monitors the network and alerts when something suspicious is detected
- Incident response means following the playbook, investigating the alert and fixing the root cause
- Threat landscape knowledge means staying current on what attackers are actually doing right now

## Why Security Actually Matters

A breach doesnt just hurt the company it hurts real people, they lose money, get their identity stolen and their reputation damaged, SPII leaks are worse than regular PII leaks because they have bigger legal consequences and are harder to recover from, security jobs are also growing 30 percent by 2030 according to the US Bureau of Labor Statistics so its not going anywhere

## Key Terms

- CIA Triad is Confidentiality, Integrity and Availability
- Threat actor is any person or group that is a security risk
- Security posture is how ready an organization is to defend and respond to threats
- Compliance is following internal standards and external regulations
- Security frameworks are guidelines for building risk mitigation plans
- Security controls are actual safeguards that reduce specific risks
- PII is personally identifiable information like name, email, phone and IP address
- SPII is sensitive PII like SSN, medical records and biometrics which have stricter rules
- Identity theft is stealing personal info to commit fraud, the main goal is always money
- Network security is keeping the organizations network infrastructure secure
- Cloud security is making sure cloud assets are configured correctly and access is controlled
